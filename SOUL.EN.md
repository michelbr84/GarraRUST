# The Soul of Soulforge

> **Every good project has a story. Only its author can tell it.**

This document is written in the first person, because Soulforge has one author and
because his answer to why it should exist was that a project is *something of its
creator's own*. Several sections below are marked as unanswered. They are left that way on
purpose — this document records what one person actually said on 3 September 2026, and
nothing else. What is missing is written down as missing, so someone can fill it in later
instead of trusting a gap that was quietly papered over.

## Origin Story

I build a personal assistant called GarraIA. It lives at
[michelbr84/GarraRUST](https://github.com/michelbr84/GarraRUST/), on my machine and in the
cloud, and I work on it most days.

Soulforge started because I wanted to show Garra to people in a warmer way than a
repository allows. Not a better feature list — a version of it someone could recognize
themselves in, close enough to how I actually think about it that people would want to be
part of it and the community around it would grow.

So the first project Soulforge was ever meant for was my own. It was built to give Garra a
soul, and turned into the thing that gives any project one.

## Why We Exist

Because if nobody writes it down, the founding vision and the author's motivation simply
are not recorded anywhere in that form. The code will survive. The reason will not.

Soulforge exists so the person who started something can put that on record, warmly, for
whoever reads it next.

## The Problem We Refuse to Accept

Garra's repository can describe Garra completely — what it does, how to run it, what it
is built with — and still give nobody a reason to care about it. Everything a repository
is good at explaining is the part that does not make anyone want to join.

That is not a documentation problem to be fixed with more documentation. It is a different
kind of writing, and almost nobody does it, because nothing in a repository asks for it and
no tool ever sits you down and asks why you started.

## Mission

Read a project, interview the person who made it, and write a `SOUL.md` that says where it
came from, who it is for, what it promises, and what it must never become. Then keep that
document honest as the project changes — amending it without erasing what it used to say,
and answering, when asked, whether a new decision still fits.

## Principles

Four, and all four came from the author rather than from reading the code. Each one is
written as the trade-off it settles, because a principle that costs nothing decides
nothing.

### The author is in command

A soul is not generated. Somebody has to answer questions, and that somebody is the person
whose project it is. So when we must choose between producing a complete, polished
document automatically and producing a shorter one that a human actually said — we choose
the one a human said, even though the automatic version would be faster and is the thing
most people will ask for first.

The other half of the same principle: what an author chooses to put in their own document
is theirs to choose. Asked what Soulforge should do if an author explicitly asked it to
invent a flattering origin story, the answer was that it should write it — *"it's their
project."* The tool's job is to make sure the author is there and deciding, not to police
what they decide.

### History is not rewritten

A project may change its mind. It does not get to pretend it never thought otherwise. So
when an amendment would read better by quietly replacing the old text, we keep the old
text — verbatim, dated, with the reason — and accept a longer, uglier document in exchange
for one that can be trusted years later.

### The essential is never behind a paywall

Writing your project's soul is the thing this exists to do, and it stays free of charge
and free of an account. So when a revenue path would require putting `SOUL.md` creation
behind a payment or a login, we close that path, even though it is the most obvious way
this could ever make money.

### Useful to the author beats principled in the abstract

Anything is welcome if it genuinely helps the user's project and pleases its author and
co-authors. So when a rule we like collides with something that would actually serve the
person whose project it is, we take their side — and we change the rule rather than quietly
ignoring it.

## Community

The point of showing Garra warmly was that people could **recognize themselves in it**,
and that the community around it would grow because of that recognition rather than in
spite of the README.

> _Open: that is the community wanted around Garra, said while explaining where Soulforge
> came from. Whether it is also the community wanted around Soulforge itself was not asked,
> and is not assumed here._

## Promise to Users

- **You will be asked.** Soulforge will not hand you a soul it wrote by itself.
- **Nothing you wrote gets deleted.** When the document changes, the old wording is kept,
  dated, with the reason.
- **The core stays free.** No payment, no account, to write your project's soul.

## What We Will Never Become

- **A soul generator.** We will never produce a finished `SOUL.md` from the code alone,
  with nobody answering anything. This is the fastest possible version of this product and
  almost certainly the most requested one. It is also the one that would make every
  document it writes worthless, so it is the one we do not build.
- **A rewriter of history.** We will never silently delete or overwrite what an earlier
  version of a soul document said.
- **A paywall around the essential.** We will never put the creation of a `SOUL.md` behind
  payment or a mandatory account.

## Deciding With This

Before a decision that shapes Soulforge, ask: **does this keep the author in command of
their own document?**

- If a feature would produce soul text without a person answering for it, it does not
  ship — however much faster it would be.
- If a change would make an earlier version of someone's document unrecoverable, it does
  not ship.
- If a revenue idea touches the writing of `SOUL.md` itself, it is closed. Revenue has to
  come from somewhere else or not at all.
- When a rule here gets in the way of genuinely helping an author, do not quietly break
  it — change it, in the open, and record what changed.
- When the answer is "the author decides," the job is to make sure the author is actually
  there deciding, and to make what they chose legible to whoever reads it later.

### A known divergence between this document and the code

As written today, the skill refuses to invent, full stop — including when an author
explicitly asks it to. The author's position, recorded above, is narrower: never write a
soul *without* the author, but what the author chooses to put in their own document is
theirs. The implementation is stricter than the soul it serves. That is a real gap, and it
is recorded here rather than resolved by quietly editing either side.
